The Bowing Guide

Twelve families of strokes used on this site. Each one shows what it looks like, where in the bow it lives, the most common mistake and which scales use it.

Middle of the bow

Détaché

Smooth, separate strokes with no gap and no accent. The bow changes direction for every note and the sound never stops.

Watch for: Bumps at each bow change. Fix: Keep the bow hand soft and let the fingers flex a tiny bit at each change.

Practice it in scales: 1, 3, 8, 16, 17, 22

Anywhere; plan the distribution

Legato (slurs)

Several notes in one bow. The left hand changes notes while the bow keeps a steady speed and contact point.

Watch for: Running out of bow, or the last notes of the slur fading. Fix: Divide the bow into equal parts before you start, and keep the speed even.

Practice it in scales: 2, 5, 7, 9, 14, 21

Middle to upper half

On-the-string staccato

Short notes with the bow staying in the string. The bow stops after every note.

Watch for: Notes that are short but scratchy. Fix: Release a little weight at the moment the bow stops, then set it again for the next note.

Practice it in scales: 4, 35, 37, 72, 77, 88

Upper half

Martelé

A hammered stroke: pre-weight the string, release with a quick bow, stop completely. The start has a consonant-like bite.

Watch for: Pressing during the note, which chokes the sound. Fix: All the weight goes in before the note. During the stroke the arm lets go.

Practice it in scales: 6, 20, 39, 53, 79, 90

Near the balance point, lower half

Spiccato

An off-the-string stroke. The bow drops and bounces in a shallow arc and the hair leaves the string between notes.

Watch for: Bouncing too high, so the rhythm becomes uneven. Fix: Lower the arm and make the arc flatter. Let the stick bounce; do not throw it.

Practice it in scales: 10, 25, 32, 43, 58, 65

At the frog

Collé

A tiny pinched stroke made mostly by the fingers. Set the bow, pinch, release, and lift.

Watch for: Using the whole arm, which makes the stroke too big. Fix: Hold the arm still and let only the fingers and wrist make the stroke.

Practice it in scales: 26, 59

Middle of the bow

Louré (portato)

Several notes in one bow, each gently separated by a small release of weight. The bow does not stop.

Watch for: Stopping the bow, which turns it into slurred staccato. Fix: Think of each note as a soft swell, like saying "da-da-da" in one breath.

Practice it in scales: 12, 31, 45, 64, 68, 89

Middle to upper half

Hooked bowing

A long and a short note in the same bow direction with a small stop between them. Used for dotted rhythms.

Watch for: The short note comes too early or too long. Fix: Count the sixteenth carefully and give it a quick, light bow.

Practice it in scales: 13, 46

Upper half for up-bow, lower half for down-bow

Slurred staccato

Several short, stopped notes in one bow direction. The bow stops, the finger changes, the bow moves again.

Watch for: Notes running together. Fix: Exaggerate the stops at first. Clarity first, speed later.

Practice it in scales: 18, 29, 51, 62

Middle of the bow

Accented détaché

Each note starts with a quick burst of bow speed, then relaxes while the bow keeps moving.

Watch for: Squeezing to make the accent. Fix: Make the accent with speed, not pressure.

Practice it in scales: 1, 3, 8, 16, 17, 22

Frog to tip

Whole bows

Use the entire bow on every note with a steady, ringing tone.

Watch for: Arriving at the tip early, then crawling. Fix: Mark the middle of the bow and check that you pass it exactly halfway through each note.

Practice it in scales: 1, 3, 8, 16, 17, 22

Varies

Bow distribution patterns

Mixed slurs and separate notes (slur 3 + 1, slur 2 + 2, and so on) train the bow to change speed while the tone stays even.

Watch for: Drifting toward the tip or the frog over several beats. Fix: Use the same amount of bow for the slur as for the separate note, which means the separate note moves faster.

Practice it in scales: 11, 15, 19, 24, 30, 44
