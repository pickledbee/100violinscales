Getting started

How to Use This Site

Scales are where tone, intonation, shifting and bow control all get built at the same time. Here is how to get the most out of every one of these 100.

Play the same notes, change the bow

Each key appears several times with a different bowing. The notes stay familiar so your attention can go to the right arm. When a new bowing feels awkward, go back to an earlier key you already know and try it there first.

Work in this order every time

1. Play the scale slowly with separate bows and listen only for pitch. 2. Add the written bowing at a slow tempo. 3. Climb the metronome a few clicks at a time toward the tempo goal. 4. Finish by playing it once beautifully, as if someone were listening.

Use the metronome ladder

Start at a tempo where every note is clean. Play the scale twice in a row without a mistake, then move the metronome up 4 clicks. If you miss, drop back 8 clicks and climb again. Log your best tempo on the scale page.

Check your intonation with ringing tones

Every G, D, A and E you play can be checked against an open string. When the note is in tune, the open string vibrates in sympathy and the violin rings. If it sounds dull, the note is not quite in tune yet.

Use the play-along wisely

Listen once before you play. Then play along softly with the click on. Finally turn the sound off and play with the metronome alone: that is the real test.

A little every day beats a lot once a week

Ten to fifteen focused minutes of scales a day will change your playing more than an hour on Saturday. The Practice Plan gives you a ready-made path through all 100.

Reading the Markings

Down-bow

Pull the bow from the frog toward the tip.

Up-bow

Push the bow from the tip toward the frog.

1 2 3 4
Finger number

0 = open string, 1 = index, 2 = middle, 3 = ring, 4 = pinky.

3rd pos.
Position

Shows which position to play in. Used in the shifting scales wherever the hand moves.

Staccato dot

Short. Stop the bow between notes (on the string unless it says spiccato or collé).

Tenuto line

Full length. Under a slur it means louré: a gentle pulse without stopping the bow.

Accent

Start the note with extra bow speed and weight, then release.

Martelé wedge

Pinch, release, stop. A biting start with silence after each note.

⌢
Slur

All notes under the curved line are played in one bow.

< >
Crescendo / diminuendo

Grow louder, or grow softer, by changing bow speed and weight.

Position by position

POSITIONWHAT TO KNOW
1stRinging-tone check: every G, D, A and E you play should make the matching open string vibrate in sympathy.
2ndIn 2nd position your 1st finger sits where your 2nd finger used to be in 1st position. Keep the thumb opposite the 1st or 2nd finger.
3rdIn 3rd position the heel of your hand lightly touches the violin body. Use that contact as your guide for every shift into 3rd.
4thIn 4th position your 1st finger plays the same note as the next string up in 1st position (open string pitch, one string higher). Check it.
5thIn 5th position the hand starts to come around the shoulder of the violin. Bring the thumb partly under the neck and keep the elbow forward.

Five rules for clean shifts

  1. Slide on the finger you are leaving. Lighten its pressure during the slide so the string stays clean.
  2. The thumb travels with the hand. Never leave it behind in the old position.
  3. Hear the new note before you arrive. Sing it in your head while you shift.
  4. Use a guide note: practice the shift slowly with the slide audible, then make it faster and quieter.
  5. Keep the bow moving at an even speed through the shift so the shift does not stick out.
